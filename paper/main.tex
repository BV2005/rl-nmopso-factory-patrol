\begin{abstract}
Multi-objective particle swarm optimisers such as NMOPSO produce high-quality Pareto fronts of UAV
paths but need minutes per leg, which confines them to offline planning. We cast NMOPSO's own
leg-planning problem---the same spherical path encoding, the same bounds and the same four objectives
(path length, threat, altitude, smoothness)---as a short-horizon Markov decision process and train a
single preference-conditioned policy with PPO. At inference the policy returns a Pareto front for an
unseen leg in milliseconds. On the 73 legs of a three-drone patrol of a 30\,$\times$\,20\,m factory
(Gazebo world \texttt{factory2}) and 200 held-out random legs, we compare against the authors'
unmodified NMOPSO, scoring both methods with the authors' cost function. \emph{[Results to be filled
in from the improvement loop.]}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Indoor patrol of factories by small multirotors requires collision-free, short and smooth paths
between patrol points that are packed between racks and machines. NMOPSO~\cite{nmopso} formulates
each leg as a four-objective problem and returns a Pareto front; in our three-drone factory patrol
pipeline (balanced power-diagram zoning, free-space lane generation, 2-opt tours) it plans every leg,
taking a median of about 300\,s per leg on one CPU core---about 50\,min for a 72-leg plan on six
workers. This rules out replanning in flight.
\emph{Contributions:} (i) an exact MDP formulation of NMOPSO's leg problem, so that a learned planner
optimises precisely the same objectives in the same decision space; (ii) a preference-conditioned
PPO planner that amortises the whole Pareto front; (iii) a fair benchmark against the unmodified
NMOPSO on real patrol legs, with all costs scored by the authors' code.

\section{Problem}
A leg runs from $s$ to $g$ in the patrol band $z\in[1.6,2.0]$\,m. A path is $n=10$ nodes
$P_1..P_n$ between $s$ and $g$, encoded as in NMOPSO by $(r_i,\psi_i,\varphi_i)$ with
$r_i\in[r_{\max}/9, r_{\max}]$, $r_{\max}=3\|g-s\|/n$, $\psi_i,\varphi_i\in[-\pi/4,\pi/4]$, decoded by
chained transforms from the start pose and clamped to the map. Obstacles are the factory's boxes,
inflated by 0.4\,m and covered by 87 vertical cylinders. The objectives are NMOPSO's
$F_1=|1-\|g-s\|/\ell|$ (path length $\ell$), $F_2$ (mean threat over segment--cylinder pairs, infinite
on collision), $F_3$ (mean altitude deviation from the band centre), $F_4$ (mean turning angle$/\pi$).

\section{Method}
\subsection{MDP}
Step $i$ selects $(r_i,\psi_i,\varphi_i)$; the state holds the chained pose. The observation is the
goal in the local frame, the clamping offset, the altitude in the band, the steps left, the leg
length, 16 range readings to inflated cylinders and map bounds (precomputed table), and the
preference $w\in\Delta^3$. The terminal reward is $-w^\top(F\oslash\sigma)$ for feasible paths and
$-(6+v)$ otherwise ($v$: soft violation); a potential-based term $\Phi=-0.5\|q-g\|/\|g-s\|$ with
$\Phi=0$ at the end shapes intermediate steps without changing the optimal policy~\cite{ng1999}.
\subsection{Training}
PPO with GAE, preferences drawn from Dirichlet(1), legs drawn between random free points of the hall.
\subsection{Pareto front at inference}
For $K$ preferences (corners, edge midpoints, centroid, random), one deterministic and $S$ sampled
paths each; the feasible non-dominated set is the front.

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Fairness}
Both methods use the identical model (exported from the planner's own \texttt{CreateModel.m}). Our
batched PyTorch port of the cost is used only for training; it matches \texttt{MyCost.m} to
$10^{-15}$ on 8000 random solutions with identical feasibility. All reported costs are re-scored by
the authors' MATLAB code. NMOPSO is run unmodified (MaxIt 500, nPop 100, nRep 50), three seeds.
\subsection{Metrics}
Per leg, objectives are normalised by the ideal and nadir of the union of all fronts; hypervolume
with reference 1.1. We also report the path the patrol pipeline would fly (\emph{short-safe}
selection) and wall-clock time.
