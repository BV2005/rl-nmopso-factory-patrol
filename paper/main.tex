\title{Reinforcement-Learned Proposals for Multi-Objective UAV Path Planning:\\
Making NMOPSO Fast Enough for Indoor Factory Patrol}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Bharath Vaaishnav}}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Multi-objective particle swarm optimisers such as NMOPSO produce high-quality Pareto fronts of UAV
paths but need minutes per leg, which confines them to offline planning. We cast NMOPSO's own
leg-planning problem---the same spherical path encoding, the same bounds and the same four objectives
(path length, threat, altitude, smoothness)---as a short-horizon Markov decision process and train a
single policy with PPO, using a residual action space whose zero action is the straight, level path
and that maps exactly into NMOPSO's encoding. The policy proposes a set of paths for an unseen leg in
0.2\,s; kept in an external archive together with a short warm-started NMOPSO run, they form the
returned front. On the 73 legs of a three-drone patrol of a 30\,$\times$\,20\,m factory (Gazebo world
\texttt{factory2}), scored with the authors' cost function against the authors' unmodified NMOPSO
(3 seeds), the RL proposals alone exceed the hypervolume of NMOPSO with 50 iterations by 8.5\,\% at
1/190 of its time, and the hybrid with 50 NMOPSO iterations exceeds the budget-matched NMOPSO by
11.4\,\% (68\,\% of runs better, 3\,\% worse). Full NMOPSO (500 iterations) given the straight path
remains 4.4\,\% better than the hybrid at ten times the cost. We also report what did not work:
preference and threat-budget conditioning were not learned by PPO in this setting.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Indoor patrol of factories by small multirotors requires collision-free, short and smooth paths
between patrol points that are packed between racks and machines. NMOPSO~\cite{nmopso} formulates
each leg as a four-objective problem and returns a Pareto front; in our three-drone factory patrol
pipeline (balanced power-diagram zoning, free-space lane generation, 2-opt tours) it plans every leg,
taking a median of about 300\,s per leg on one CPU core---about 50\,min for a 72-leg plan on six
workers. This rules out replanning in flight.
\emph{Contributions:} (i) an exact MDP formulation of NMOPSO's leg problem, so that a learned planner
optimises precisely the same objectives in the same decision space; (ii) a preference-conditioned
PPO planner that amortises the whole Pareto front; (iii) a fair benchmark against the unmodified
NMOPSO on real patrol legs, with all costs scored by the authors' code.

\section{Problem}
A leg runs from $s$ to $g$ in the patrol band $z\in[1.6,2.0]$\,m. A path is $n=10$ nodes
$P_1..P_n$ between $s$ and $g$, encoded as in NMOPSO by $(r_i,\psi_i,\varphi_i)$ with
$r_i\in[r_{\max}/9, r_{\max}]$, $r_{\max}=3\|g-s\|/n$, $\psi_i,\varphi_i\in[-\pi/4,\pi/4]$, decoded by
chained transforms from the start pose and clamped to the map. Obstacles are the factory's boxes,
inflated by 0.4\,m and covered by 87 vertical cylinders. The objectives are NMOPSO's
$F_1=|1-\|g-s\|/\ell|$ (path length $\ell$), $F_2$ (mean threat over segment--cylinder pairs, infinite
on collision), $F_3$ (mean altitude deviation from the band centre), $F_4$ (mean turning angle$/\pi$).

\section{Method}
\subsection{MDP}
Step $i$ selects $(r_i,\psi_i,\varphi_i)$; the state holds the chained pose. The observation is the
goal in the local frame, the clamping offset, the altitude in the band, the steps left, the leg
length, 16 range readings to inflated cylinders and map bounds (precomputed table), and the
preference $w\in\Delta^3$. The terminal reward is $-w^\top(F\oslash\sigma)$ for feasible paths and
$-(6+v)$ otherwise ($v$: soft violation); a potential-based term $\Phi=-0.5\|q-g\|/\|g-s\|$ with
$\Phi=0$ at the end shapes intermediate steps without changing the optimal policy~\cite{ng1999}.
\subsection{Training}
PPO with GAE, preferences drawn from Dirichlet(1), legs drawn between random free points of the hall.
\subsection{Pareto front at inference}
For $K$ preferences (corners, edge midpoints, centroid, random), one deterministic and $S$ sampled
paths each; the feasible non-dominated set is the front.

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Fairness}
Both methods use the identical model (exported from the planner's own \texttt{CreateModel.m}). Our
batched PyTorch port of the cost is used only for training; it matches \texttt{MyCost.m} to
$10^{-15}$ on 8000 random solutions with identical feasibility. All reported costs are re-scored by
the authors' MATLAB code. NMOPSO is run unmodified (MaxIt 500, nPop 100, nRep 50), three seeds.
\subsection{Metrics}
Per leg, objectives are normalised by the ideal and nadir of the union of all fronts; hypervolume
with reference 1.1. We also report the path the patrol pipeline would fly (\emph{short-safe}
selection) and wall-clock time.

\subsection{Path quality and robustness}
For the path each method would fly (short-safe pick; \texttt{scripts/robustness.py}, 73 legs
$\times$ 3 seeds, physical boxes, drone radius 0.3\,m): RL paths are 1.10$\times$ the straight
distance against 1.36$\times$ for NMOPSO-500 (mean 3.44 vs 4.02\,m; on the whole patrol, drone 1's
loop is 75.6 vs 115.1\,m), with similar interior clearance (5th percentile 0.94 vs 0.90\,m). No
deployed path of any method collides nominally or with obstacles up to 0.3\,m larger than modelled.
With Gaussian waypoint errors the collision probability is 0 at $\sigma=0.1$\,m for all methods and
2.2\,\% (RL) vs 0.6\,\% (NMOPSO-500) at $\sigma=0.3$\,m, where RL's straight flight down 2\,m rack
aisles keeps every waypoint near the racks. On 200 random legs up to 15\,m, RL finds no feasible path
on 11\,\% (19\,\% beyond 11\,m); NMOPSO cannot start on 5\,\% (its initialisation resamples until a
random path is feasible) and starts with probability below 0.5 on 22.5\,\%.
\emph{Unexpected obstacle} (\texttt{scripts/replan\_obstacle.py}): a 0.6\,m object placed at the
midpoint of 37 legs blocks 86--89\,\% of the planned paths. Replanning succeeds on the same 27 legs
for all methods; the other 10 are blocked in the planner's model (aisle gaps of 0.65--0.85\,m) and
NMOPSO never starts on them. RL replans in 1.2\,s and beats NMOPSO-50 + straight (38\,s) on 69\,\%
of runs (ties 27\,\%, losses 4\,\%), with paths 1.29 vs 1.60$\times$ the straight distance.

\subsection{Gazebo / PX4 SITL flights}
Three x500 drones fly the factory2\_auto\_full patrol (pads at $(-8,7)$, $(-4,7)$, $(0,7)$, one lap,
1\,m/s) with the demo's follower and recorder. A first flight of the plan as generated ended in a
mid-air collision of drones 2 and 3 over drone 3's pad: the layout routes drone 2's transit across

\subsection{What did not work}
Preference conditioning (linear scalarisation) and threat-budget conditioning ($F_2\le u\,F_2^{\text{straight}}$,
with penalties up to 50 per $\sigma_2$) were not learned: the mean action changed by $<0.03$ across the
full range of the conditioning input. The low-threat end of the front therefore comes from NMOPSO.
NMOPSO's own repository discards warm-start paths (grid-based deletion), which is why the external
archive matters.
